# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management guidance for OctoAcme. The documentation follows a practical lifecycle that helps teams move from idea to delivery in a structured, repeatable way. The process emphasizes clear ownership, measurable outcomes, regular communication, iterative delivery, and continuous learning.

## Project management lifecycle

OctoAcme begins every initiative with project initiation to validate the business need, identify stakeholders, align on goals, and decide whether a project should move forward. Once approved, teams move into planning, where scope is broken into prioritized backlog items, milestones are created, dependencies are identified, and delivery responsibilities are assigned. During execution, the team works in small, testable increments through a project board, pull request reviews, QA validation, and regular status communication. Release and deployment activities focus on readiness, smoke testing, rollout, and rollback planning. After each milestone or release, teams capture lessons learned through retrospectives and turn them into actionable improvements.

## Roles and personas

OctoAcme relies on clear role definitions to keep accountability and communication aligned. Project Managers coordinate delivery, timing, risks, and stakeholder updates. Product Managers define the problem, prioritize work, and validate value. Developers build and test software, maintain documentation, and contribute to design and review quality. QA and testing roles validate acceptance criteria and production readiness. Stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context throughout the project.

## Communication and quality approach

Communication is structured around regular check-ins and transparent status updates. Teams use daily standups, weekly syncs, milestone reviews, and ad hoc escalation when risks impact delivery. Risks and dependencies are tracked in a register, reviewed regularly, and escalated through defined ownership paths when needed. Quality is built into the process through automated CI checks, testing requirements, security scanning, manual QA where needed, and acceptance criteria tied to the definition of done.
